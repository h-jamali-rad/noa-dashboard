    Cognitive biases applicable to medical decisions [9]

    • Aggregate bias/Ecological fallacy: average is assumed to represent an individual
    • Anchoring: premature hypothesis formation
    • Ascertainment bias: prejudice, seeing what you expect to find
    • Availability and nonavailability: bias toward things that come readily to mind
    • Base-rate neglect: failure to account for prevalence of disease
    • Commission bias: tendency toward action over inaction (often due to pressure from others)
    • Confirmation bias: tendency to notice evidence which confirms earlier hypotheses
    • Diagnosis momentum: diagnosis without adequate evidence due to many people's belief in it
    • Feedback sanction: continue to make error if the feedback is delayed (or doesn't happen)
    • Framing effect
    • Fundamental attribution error: tendency to blame people rather than circumstances
    • Gambler's fallacy: tendency to believe a sequence will not continue
    • Gender bias
    • Hindsight bias
    • Multiple alternatives bias: bias toward the status quo if there are too many options
    • Omission bias: tendency toward inaction over action (more common than commission bias)
    • Order effects: tendency to remember the beginning and end of a story and not the middle
    • Outcome bias: belief that a good outcome is more likely, influence of personal desires
    • Overconfidence bias: acting on incomplete information and hunches
    • Playing the odds: undue attention to (perceived) common things, ignoring rare things
    • Posterior probability error: assumption that this case will be like past ones
    • Premature closure: accepting a diagnoses which is not fully verified
    • Psych-out error: errors related to patients with psychiatric conditions
    • Representativeness restraint: assumption that all cases are typical
    • Search satisfying: tendency to stop searching once an answer is found
    • Sutton's slip: going for the obvious (results in ignoring risks, comorbidities, or more rare cause)
    • Sunk costs
    • Triage-cueing: the initial assessment of severity (at triage) will tend to persist
    • Unpacking principle: things described more explicitly are judged to be more likely
    • Vertical line failure: inflexible thinking, inability to correctly deal with unusual cases
    • Visceral bias: emotional influence on decisions; the effect of one's like or dislike of a patient
    • Yin-Yang out: tendency to be less thorough if patient recently had an extensive workup
    • Zebra retreat: failure to pursue zebra diagnoses

    New errors introduced by CPOE [8]

    • substitution errors: select something next to the thing you meant
    • selecting wrong patient (can't tell which patient file is open)
    • system delays in transferring information (sending does not confirm receipt)
    • inability to view all relevant information
    • inflexible systems (does not allow non-standard orders)
    • interpreting default = recommendation
    • interpreting absence of alert = all is well
